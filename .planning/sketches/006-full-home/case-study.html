ContextSection 01

By the time we started, Moodle was already the most-used LMS in the world. That wasn't the problem. The problem was that most of those users dreaded opening it — and many had stopped showing up to class because of it.

The product had grown by accretion. Each year a new feature, a new committee, a new modal. Six versions of the same button living in three places. The team had been patching the surface for so long that no one remembered the room underneath.

The ProblemSection 02

When the LMS feels like a filing cabinet, students stop showing up.

The first round of research interviews returned the same observation, almost verbatim, from twelve different students at five different institutions: "I just don't know where anything is." Course pages had become storage. Discussion forums lived three clicks deep. Submitting an assignment required confidence.

We mapped the existing navigation against the actual journeys students took, and the gap was — embarrassingly — the size of the product itself.

ResearchSection 03

Six weeks of listening.

47 students. 12 instructors. 5 institutional admins. We sat with each one, opened Moodle, and watched.

The signal was deafeningly consistent. The problem wasn't in any one place; it was the cumulative weight of a hundred small frictions, each of which had a perfectly reasonable explanation at the time it was added.

Most digital products feel
assembled. The work was
to make this one feel
made.
ApproachSection 04

Editing, not adding.

We approached the redesign as a writing problem before a design problem. The team had already collected a stack of "feature ideas" three inches deep. We threw out the stack.

Instead, we asked what could be removed. What could be hidden until needed. What could be combined. The first prototype had 31% fewer surfaces. The second had 44% fewer.

SolutionSection 05

A calmer interface, by subtraction.

The new course landing surface puts the next action — the thing the student actually needs — at the top of the page. Everything else is one tap away, not three.
